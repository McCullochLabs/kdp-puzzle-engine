# Marketplace Merchandising

Marketplace copy and assets must reflect how each channel presents products.

## Amazon KDP

Use accurate title/subtitle, physical-book description, seven distinct backend keyword phrases, appropriate categories, verified count/difficulty/format, and a post-publication A+ plan. Never invent rank, demand, sales, competition, or approval.

## Etsy

Treat Etsy as a visual merchandising marketplace. A digital listing needs a readable title, 13 concise tags, use cases, download instructions, and an unmistakable **DIGITAL DOWNLOAD — NO PHYSICAL PRODUCT** disclosure.

The local Etsy compositor creates ten images from the real approved front cover and real completed interior:

1. Hero/thumbnail
2. What You Get
3. Interior Puzzle Preview
4. Second Interior Preview
5. Solution Preview
6. Readability/Large Print Preview
7. Puzzle/Page Count
8. Print-at-Home/PDF Instructions
9. Audience/Use Case
10. Digital Download disclosure

It fails closed when cover approval is missing or stale, when the real puzzle or solution page cannot be identified, or when the completed files are missing. It never edits the production PDFs or fabricates sample pages.

## Payhip

Use branded landing-page copy, SEO metadata, a gallery plan, related products, collections, and cross-sells. The customer-safe digital file and store/product setup sheets remain the operational authorities. Payhip copy should not be a verbatim Etsy clone.

## Bookstore and distribution channels

Barnes & Noble Press and IngramSpark require professional bibliographic metadata, accurate audience/edition information, relevant subject classification, and printer-specific files. Etsy-style promotional language is inappropriate here.

## Lulu, Bookvault, and direct storefronts

Treat these primarily as fulfillment or seller-led direct-commerce routes. Describe product specifications, cross-sells, bundles, and fulfillment accurately. Do not imply Amazon- or Etsy-like organic discovery.
